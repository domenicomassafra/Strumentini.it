# Brand, naming and go-to-market lane — 2026-09-23

Status: historical naming research retained for decision history. StrumentiUtili.it is now the selected public brand; this document does not constitute legal clearance.

## Executive summary

The product should not be branded as a giant utility catalog, an “OS,” a generic AI assistant, or a freelancer-only product. Existing research already shows that tool count, local/browser execution, favorites, command palettes, workflows and profession packs all exist elsewhere. The more defensible product story is the combination: a browser-native utility workspace that starts small, configures itself around a person's work, reuses safe context across deterministic tools, and lets the person add/remove portable packs without becoming a heavy productivity suite.

That points to a brand territory of quiet readiness for small jobs rather than breadth. The strongest message should make the old behavior vivid — search for another tiny site, re-enter routine context, repeat — and make the mechanism specific: a small configured set of utilities with safe defaults and installable packs.

Naming is unusually crowded. This lane generated more than 70 candidates and performed explicit collision notes on more than 40 serious candidates. Most attractive short names were already occupied. Ten names remain worth a second clearance round: Plerova, Tivanoa, Lervio, Tevalo, Niveli, Ralvoa, Seviro, Taveli, Sivela and Nolda. Plerova and Tivanoa had the cleanest observed technical namespace in the merged passes; Lervio, Tevalo, Niveli and Ralvoa were the next-lowest observed software-collision group. None is “clear.” Plerova and Tivanoa showed no RDAP object for the exact .com or .app at the time checked; exact .com domains were registered for the other eight.

The three differentiated positioning territories are:

1. Ready Small Jobs — lead with tools already set up for recurring little tasks.
2. Personal Workbench — lead with a small workbench assembled around how the person works.
3. Calm Utility Layer — lead with getting in, finishing the job locally when possible, and leaving.

The launch model should be tool → workspace → pack → repeat use → shared pack, not “launch a directory.” Individual utility pages acquire intent; the workspace earns retention; public packs create referral; open source creates technical trust/distribution.

This is a preliminary knockout screen, not a legal trademark opinion.

A no-object response from RDAP is a point-in-time observation, not a registrar guarantee.

## 2. Durable PRODUCT-MARKETING-CONTEXT equivalent

### Product

A web-native personal utility workspace for knowledge work. It starts with a small set of relevant tools, reuses safe profile defaults across compatible utilities, supports installable/removable professional packs, and keeps normal operations deterministic/client-side when technically sensible.

The catalog is supply. The product is the personal configured layer over that supply.

### Audiences

Primary research wedge:

- independent consultants;
- freelancers;
- solopreneurs;
- professional creators;
- small-agency operators;
- adjacent knowledge workers repeatedly handling files, tables, text, dates, URLs, images, pricing and lightweight calculations.

Expansion should remain credible to researchers, marketers, developers and broader knowledge workers. The public brand must therefore not encode freelancer, developer, Italy, AI, or a single feature family.

### Jobs to be done

Core verbs:

1. prepare;
2. convert;
3. clean;
4. compare;
5. calculate;
6. verify/prove where useful.

User-level job:

> When a small work task interrupts my main work, help me finish it quickly without searching for another random site, re-entering routine context, or adopting a heavy system.

### Pains

- repeated search for tiny utilities;
- context switching between unrelated sites;
- re-entering locale, timezone, currency, date/file format, rate or workweek assumptions;
- uncertainty about what random utility sites do with files;
- giant catalogs that shift discovery work onto the user;
- heavyweight productivity products that create workflows to maintain;
- probabilistic AI used where a deterministic transform or calculation would be simpler and auditable.

### Alternatives

Direct or near-direct:

- ToopTools;
- SolveKit;
- FreelTools;
- TinyWow;
- Omni Calculator;
- ToolDock;
- smarterfaster.

Mental-model alternatives:

- Raycast and extension launchers;
- Setapp and app curation;
- browser search plus one-off utility sites;
- spreadsheets and manual formulas;
- AI assistants for tasks that may not require probabilistic output;
- bookmarks;
- doing the task manually inside the source application.

### Differentiation hypothesis

No individual bullet below is unique. The hypothesis is the combination:

- no-install web access;
- role/task-aware onboarding;
- generated starter toolbox;
- shared safe Work Profile;
- executable installable/removable packs;
- deterministic/local normal path;
- persistent small workspace;
- structured compatible handoff between tools;
- portable/exportable setup;
- optional account mainly for sync.

The least pasteable mechanism is: the small workspace is configured around the person and shares safe defaults across real utilities.

Competitors can much more easily paste “fast,” “private,” “200+ tools,” “workspace,” “local,” “packs,” or “flows.”

### Objections

| Objection | Answer or proof required |
| --- | --- |
| Why not just Google the tool? | Demonstrate faster repeat use from workspace and fewer repeated inputs. |
| Why not use an AI assistant? | Show deterministic/local tasks where exact transforms, visible inputs and stable output are preferable. |
| I do not need another productivity app. | No project system to maintain; tools finish jobs and get out of the way. |
| A profile sounds invasive. | Make safe fields explicit, optional and portable; keep sensitive files local where possible. |
| Packs sound like another catalog. | Start with 8–15 useful tools and make removal/addition trivial. |
| Free utility sites already exist. | Prove personal setup and repeat-use convenience, not raw availability. |
| If it is open source, why use hosted? | Hosted convenience can coexist with portable/open deterministic layers. |

### Anti-fit

- teams shopping for CRM, ERP or project management;
- buyers who want enterprise workflow orchestration;
- users seeking primarily conversational AI;
- workflows dependent on deep proprietary integrations rather than portable browser utilities;
- single-use visitors who never expect to reuse tools — still valid acquisition traffic, but not proof of the workspace thesis;
- users expecting legal, tax, medical or financial professional judgment from generic calculators.

### Proof points available now

These are product/research facts, not customer outcomes:

- architecture target is static/browser-first and low-ops;
- no LLM is intended for the normal deterministic path;
- files should remain local whenever technically possible;
- anonymous first use and optional sign-in are explicit requirements;
- tool packs, Work Profile, portability and structured handoff are explicit product hypotheses;
- competitive research has already falsified weak differentiators such as raw tool count and privacy-only positioning.

Missing proof:

- time saved by shared defaults;
- improved time-to-right-tool versus a catalog;
- 7-day and 30-day workspace repeat use;
- whether users understand and value packs;
- whether shared pack links produce installs;
- willingness to pay for any later sync/history/team layer.

### Customer language to test

Prefer:

- small jobs;
- everyday work tools;
- already set up;
- my tools;
- add this tool;
- install this pack;
- finish it here;
- runs in your browser, where true;
- no signup required, where true.

Avoid as masterbrand language:

- 200+ tools;
- all-in-one;
- AI-powered;
- operating system;
- revolutionize, supercharge, unlock productivity;
- workflow automation platform;
- freelancer OS;
- vague privacy adjectives without explaining what stays local.

### Brand voice

- calm rather than breathless;
- precise rather than magical;
- practical rather than aspirational;
- compact but not cryptic;
- visibly technical when proof matters;
- willing to say what a tool does not do;
- low ceremony;
- no generic SaaS gradient/sparkle language as the identity.

### Business and brand objective

Near term: prove that utility-page acquisition can convert into a persistent personal workspace that people reuse.

Brand objective: create a memorable umbrella for many small utilities without looking like a tool farm and without encoding a narrow profession or technology.

Primary conversion action to test:

> Add this to my tools

Pack-level action:

> Install this pack

## 3. Naming brief

### Name requirements

The public name should:

- work in English and Italian and remain plausible internationally;
- be easy to say after seeing it once;
- avoid OS, AI, Tool, Kit, Box and Work unless exceptional;
- support an abstract modular icon rather than wrench/toolbox literalism;
- not imply one profession;
- not imply one current feature;
- feel compatible with quiet precision, readiness and personal fit;
- work in product grammar such as “[Name] pack”, “[Name] profile” and “add to [Name]”.

### Naming territories explored

1. Invented / abstract — highest priority because literal utility naming is saturated.
2. Readiness / fit — something prepared for the person.
3. Precision / craft — calibration, joinery and measurement metaphors.
4. Small personal surface — nook, tray, ledge, bench-like metaphors.
5. Completion / calm motion — finish, set, ready, clean, flow.
6. Short compounds — explored sparingly; most became descriptive or crowded.

## 6. Positioning territories

### Weighted Marketing AGI rubric

- product-thesis fit — 25;
- competitor distinctiveness / pasteability resistance — 25;
- immediate clarity — 20;
- proofability with planned product — 15;
- expansion durability — 15.

### Territory A — Ready Small Jobs

Score: 91/100

Breakdown: fit 24/25 · distinctiveness 23/25 · clarity 19/20 · proofability 13/15 · durability 12/15.

Hero:

> The small tools behind your work, already set up.

Subhead:

> Start with a compact toolbox shaped around what you do. Reuse safe defaults across files, tables, text, dates and calculations, then add or remove packs whenever your work changes.

Proof points:

- onboarding creates a small starter set rather than a 200-card homepage;
- safe Work Profile fields can prefill compatible utilities;
- normal deterministic tools run in the browser where practical;
- packs are installable/removable, not just category pages;
- setup can be portable/exportable.

CTA:

> Build my toolbox

Why it beats “200+ tools”:

Breadth makes the user do discovery. This territory sells less searching and less repeated setup. The catalog can grow while the user's surface stays intentionally small.

Attracts:

Consultants, operators, creators and generalists who repeatedly solve many tiny digital tasks and resent hunting for the same kinds of helpers.

Risk:

“Already set up” is proof-heavy. If onboarding does not create meaningfully different, useful workspaces, this positioning collapses.

### Territory B — Personal Workbench

Score: 88/100

Breakdown: fit 24/25 · distinctiveness 22/25 · clarity 17/20 · proofability 13/15 · durability 12/15.

Hero:

> A workbench for the little jobs your real work creates.

Subhead:

> Keep the utilities you actually use in one small, personal space. Install role packs, carry safe preferences between compatible tools, and remove anything that does not earn its place.

Proof points:

- home targets roughly 8–15 installed tools rather than a wall of categories;
- the full library stays secondary to Your Tools;
- packs install executable utilities;
- compatible outputs can hand structured context to a next tool;
- no project-management system is required.

CTA:

> Set up my workbench

Why it beats “200+ tools”:

It reframes the product from inventory to curation, arrangement and repeat use. The value is not owning more tools; it is having the right few within reach.

Attracts:

People who like controllable, instrument-panel software and want ownership over their setup rather than an algorithmic assistant.

Risk:

“Workbench” can drift into developer/maker branding or literal toolbox visuals. Keep the product examples rooted in broad knowledge work.

### Territory C — Calm Utility Layer

Score: 82/100

Breakdown: fit 22/25 · distinctiveness 21/25 · clarity 15/20 · proofability 13/15 · durability 11/15.

Hero:

> Finish the little job. Get back to the big one.

Subhead:

> A quiet layer of browser utilities for the interruptions between real tasks — configured around your work, local when practical, and there without another workflow to maintain.

Proof points:

- anonymous first use;
- no LLM in the normal deterministic path;
- sensitive files local where technically possible;
- no ERP/CRM/project-management ceremony;
- command/search is navigation, not the core product claim.

CTA:

> Try a tool

Why it beats “200+ tools”:

It competes against interruption cost, not catalog size. More tools can actually hurt the promise unless the personal surface remains calm.

Attracts:

Privacy-sensitive and tool-fatigued knowledge workers; technically literate users; people skeptical of AI-first productivity products.

Risk:

Emotionally distinct but less category-clear. It needs concrete product examples immediately below the hero.

## 7. How to choose positioning without guessing

Do not choose from copy preference alone.

Test:

- A: Ready Small Jobs;
- B: Personal Workbench;
- C: qualitative counter-position in interviews/usability sessions.

Behavioral evidence:

- time to first successful utility;
- five-second comprehension;
- starter-tool adds/removals;
- first-session second-tool use;
- 7-day return to workspace;
- share of returning utility launches from workspace versus search landing;
- pack installs/shares.

## 8. Launch and growth loop

### Core loop

High-intent tool → immediate value → Add this to my tools → generated workspace → repeat use → install pack → share pack → recipient previews/installs.

Every acquisition surface should feed this loop. A page that gains search traffic but never creates repeat workspace usage is acquisition without product advantage.

### Individual tool acquisition

Prioritize utilities involving:

- files;
- structured or multi-value input;
- transforms;
- comparisons;
- tables/CSV/PDF;
- visible methodology or formulas;
- reusable profile context;
- a credible next compatible tool.

Commodity one-number calculators can exist, but should not dominate investment.

Each page should:

1. work before signup;
2. explain exactly what happens to data/files;
3. expose assumptions/formulas where relevant;
4. have one workspace bridge: Add this to my tools;
5. suggest a pack only after value is delivered.

### Role / pack pages

Initial pack surfaces:

- consultants;
- independent/freelance work;
- marketers;
- researchers;
- creators;
- developers/data.

Guardrail: each page must install a real starter pack. Do not create thin “for role” pages whose only job is keyword coverage.

### Shareable packs

The referral object should be a setup, not a coupon.

Example:

> My client-handoff pack → preview utilities/layout → install → personalize.

Measure:

- pack preview to install;
- install to first utility success;
- sender-to-recipient spread;
- downstream removals/additions.

### Open source / GitHub

Strong OSS candidates:

- utility manifest/schema;
- deterministic utility engine;
- selected utilities;
- test vectors and formula fixtures;
- contribution template;
- pack format.

OSS should prove inspectability and durability, not act as a vague badge.

Potential flywheel:

contributor adds trustworthy utility → catalog gains supply → pack includes it → utility page gains users → contributors see demand.

### Community distribution

Distribute specific utilities or packs where there is a real reason to care.

Good message shapes:

- a local CSV normalizer with specific preservation behavior;
- a consultant estimate → margin → handoff pack;
- a PDF operation that stays in-browser with implementation notes and limits.

Bad shape:

> We launched 200 free tools.

That is indistinguishable from tool-farm promotion.

### Product Hunt viability

Viable later, not as a research-stage directory.

Current Product Hunt guidance accessed 2026-09-23 says:

- products should be live/usable and high quality;
- 2026 featuring guidance looks for usefulness plus novelty, craft or creativity;
- makers can and are encouraged to post their own product;
- Product Hunt discourages asking directly for upvotes and inauthentic promotion;
- scheduling around 12:01am Pacific gives the full daily cycle, while Product Hunt itself warns there is no magic “best day.”

Launch story to test:

> A utility workspace that starts with the tools you need instead of showing you 200.

Demo sequence:

1. choose role/context;
2. watch the initial workspace change;
3. reuse one safe default across multiple deterministic tools;
4. install a pack;
5. share/import a pack.

Primary Product Hunt metric:

> activated workspaces with a second utility successfully used

Do not use leaderboard rank as the product metric.

### Hacker News / Show HN viability

Good fit only when the product is genuinely usable and the technical story is real.

Current Show HN guidance accessed 2026-09-23 says:

- people must be able to try or play with what was made;
- it should be non-trivial;
- barriers such as signup/email should be avoided where possible;
- do not ask friends to upvote or comment;
- landing pages and reading-only material are not Show HN;
- HN currently warns about a large influx of Show HNs and asks people to learn the community before posting.

Strong HN angle:

- browser-native deterministic execution;
- local-file handling;
- open manifest/engine;
- no unnecessary LLM;
- portable personal configuration;
- concrete technical tradeoffs.

Weak HN angle:

- polished all-in-one productivity marketing;
- a list of hundreds of tools;
- AI-generated founder story;
- growth-hacked engagement.

### Newsletter and content

Newsletter is not a launch prerequisite.

Use only after there is recurring product supply worth curating:

> 3 small tools worth adding this month.

Higher-priority content:

- pack pages;
- transparent calculation/methodology pages;
- practical workflow explainers;
- new-tool changelog;
- user-created pack showcases;
- technical local-first and OSS notes.

Do not build an AI-written daily blog.

## 9. Three highest-leverage growth experiments

### Experiment 1 — tool page to workspace

Hypothesis:

If a successful tool result is followed by Add this to my tools plus short relevance onboarding, more users will activate a personal workspace than with a generic See all tools CTA because the next step preserves the job they just completed.

Primary metric: successful tool users who activate a workspace.

Guardrail: original tool completion rate must not fall.

Reject if: onboarding abandonment cancels the activation lift.

### Experiment 2 — generated pack versus self-browse

Hypothesis:

If new users receive an 8–12 tool role-aware starter set, time-to-second-use and 7-day return should improve versus opening the full catalog because discovery work is reduced.

Primary metric: 7-day workspace return.

Guardrail: removal rate and wrong-tool feedback.

Reject if: self-browse users return equally or more often, or generated sets require heavy cleanup.

### Experiment 3 — shared pack referral

Hypothesis:

If users share a useful named pack with a preview, recipients will activate at a higher rate than visitors to a generic homepage because the sender supplies context and curation.

Primary metric: pack-preview to first successful utility use.

Guardrail: spam and low-quality public packs.

Reject if: recipients inspect but do not install/use, implying packs are not a meaningful referral object.

## 10. Brand risk: SEO tool farm and AI-slop failure modes

### What would make the project look like a tool farm

- hero talks about number of utilities;
- full catalog dominates first run;
- hundreds of near-identical keyword pages are generated;
- role pages contain generic prose but no genuinely different pack;
- every tool has templated long-form SEO filler;
- categories are duplicated to target keywords;
- ads overpower the utility;
- formulas/transform behavior are inconsistent or unversioned;
- tools upload files without explaining why;
- cross-linking exists for SEO rather than task continuation.

### What would make it look like AI slop

- AI inserted into deterministic jobs for novelty;
- sparkle/robot/brain visual language;
- smooth but empty copy;
- synthetic testimonials or “trusted by” claims;
- invented usage metrics;
- AI-generated daily content with no first-hand method, data or tooling value;
- hidden probabilistic behavior in tasks users expect to be exact.

### Counter-design

- Home = Your Tools, Recent, Suggested additions, Search; library secondary.
- Publish fewer, better utilities with tests and visible methodology.
- Every role page installs a concrete pack.
- Every SEO page must be a functioning product surface first.
- Prefer exact data-handling copy over “privacy-first” adjectives.
- Keep deterministic outputs deterministic.
- Mark AI-dependent tools as a different capability class if they ever exist.
- Allow removal; curation and scarcity are part of the brand.
- Put source/method/version metadata on calculators/transforms where it matters.
- Measure repeat workspace use rather than indexed-page count.

### Advertising risk

A full-free plus ads strategy can support acquisition only if ads do not recreate the low-trust utility-site aesthetic.

If ads are ever tested:

- no interstitial before task completion;
- no deceptive download buttons;
- no ad adjacent to destructive/file-action controls;
- do not sell privacy while using opaque tracking;
- preserve a clean no-signup path;
- consider optional ad-free support only later.

5. App-store/social-handle check repeated immediately before selection.
6. Visual stress test at favicon, command palette, mobile header and GitHub org sizes.
7. Owner selects from evidence; this lane does not select the public brand.
