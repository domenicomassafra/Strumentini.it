# Brand and positioning brief

## Brand status

Strumentini.it is the selected public brand.

Historical research found a collision involving the former codename. That finding is retained as decision history; Strumentini.it is the selected replacement identity.

## Brand idea

**Personal relevance, not breadth.**

The product should feel like:

- a small workbench chosen for you;
- utilities that already know safe defaults;
- fast work without hunting random sites;
- one place for little jobs.

## Positioning draft

For independent knowledge workers who constantly jump between tiny online utilities, Strumentini.it is a personal work-utility workspace that starts with the tools relevant to your job and keeps them ready with your safe defaults.

Unlike generic tool directories, it is person-first: you begin with a small configured workspace, not hundreds of cards.

## Message hierarchy

1. Relevance — only the tools you actually need.
2. Ready — common defaults are already set.
3. Speed — finish tiny jobs without hunting.
4. Privacy — compatible tools run locally.
5. Control — add, remove and export your setup.

Privacy is a proof point, not the hero claim.

## Naming brief

The selected public name, Strumentini.it, should continue to:

- be short and pronounceable in English and Italian;
- not contain OS;
- ideally avoid literal Tool, Box, Kit and Work compounds because that namespace is crowded;
- not lock the brand to freelancers;
- support a simple domain and abstract icon;
- survive web, app-store, package-registry and trademark screening.

Brand verification checklist:

1. verify exact web/domain ownership and canonical origin;
2. verify GitHub/npm/PyPI/App Store/Play Store namespace conflicts where relevant;
3. run EUIPO/USPTO/UKIPO initial trademark screening where required;
4. verify Italian and English pronunciation/readability;
5. verify social handles where they matter.

Do not silently replace Strumentini.it with a new public name without an explicit owner decision.

## Visual direction

Avoid generic SaaS gradients and literal toolbox/wrench imagery.

Explore:

- quiet utility;
- grid/slots/modules;
- small precise glyphs;
- desk/workbench metaphors;
- calm typography with high information density;
- strong distinction between installed tools and the full library.

The product should feel like an instrument panel, not a content site.
